You write a careful 155-character meta description, ship it, and a week later the search result shows something else entirely — a sentence pulled from the middle of your third paragraph.

This is normal. Google rewrites descriptions on the majority of queries, and studies consistently put it somewhere around 60–70%. It is not a penalty and it does not mean your description was bad.

## Why it happens

A meta description is static. A search query is not.

If your page about running shoes has one description, but people arrive via "waterproof trail shoes size 11", "best running shoes flat feet" and "cheap running trainers", no single sentence serves all three. So Google generates a snippet that contains the words the searcher actually typed, because a snippet with bolded query terms gets clicked more.

The rewrite is Google optimising for click-through on *that query*. Your description is a default, not a contract.

## When your description is used verbatim

It still matters, and in these cases it is usually what appears:

- **Brand and navigational searches.** Someone searching your company name gets your homepage description.
- **Exact-match queries.** If the search closely matches your description's topic, there is nothing to improve on.
- **Social and chat previews.** WhatsApp, Slack, LinkedIn and X read your tags directly. They do not rewrite anything.
- **Pages with thin or awkward body copy.** With nothing good to extract, Google falls back to what you wrote.

That last point cuts both ways: a page Google *can* extract from will often be extracted from.

## What to do instead of fighting it

**Write for the brand query.** Assume the description will appear when someone already knows who you are. Make it the sentence you would want a returning visitor to read.

**Do not stuff keywords into it.** Keywords in the description are not a ranking factor. They are only bolded when they match the query, and Google will do that extraction itself if the terms appear in your body copy.

**Check the pixel width, not the character count.** Google truncates on rendered width. A description full of capitals and wide characters is cut well before 160 characters. Our [meta tag generator](/tools/meta-tag-generator) measures the actual pixel width and shows where the cut falls.

**Make sure your body copy is extractable.** Since Google will often build a snippet from your page, short declarative sentences near the top of each section give it something good to use.

## The one case worth worrying about

If Google is rewriting your description into something *wrong* — pulling a cookie banner, a navigation label, or boilerplate from your footer — that is a real signal. It usually means the crawler cannot find substantive content near the top of your page.

Fix the page, not the tag.
